The Rule That Actually Fails People In Prop Challenges

You did not fail your prop challenge because your strategy is bad.

You failed because on day 12 you were already 80% through today's loss allowance and you took another trade anyway. The firm does not fail you for losing. They fail you for losing too much in one day.

Two rules, not one

Daily loss limit resets every day. Usually around 5% of the account.

Max drawdown is total, over the whole account. Usually around 10%.

Fail either and the account is gone. Most people track the big one and ignore the daily one, and the daily one is what actually kills them — because it resets, so it feels less serious.

Why it surprises people

A breach is never sudden. It is always a slow slide. You take a loss in the morning, size up slightly on the next one because you are sure, take a third because now you are annoyed, and by lunch you have used 90% of a limit you did not know you were close to.

The number was there the whole time. Nobody was watching it.

What to do instead

Before every trade, know three numbers:

  • How much of today's loss allowance is left
  • How much total drawdown is left
  • The exact position size that keeps one loss to a fixed percentage

If you cannot answer all three in ten seconds, you are guessing. Guessing is what ends accounts.

The rule I use now

If I have used 80% of today's allowance, I stop. Not one more trade. Not "this setup is different". Stop.

It is not about the trade. It is about not giving the firm a reason to fail you.

The uncomfortable part

None of this is exciting. There is no indicator here. It is bookkeeping.

But the traders who pass challenges are not the ones with the best entries. They are the ones who are still in the game on day 20. That is a risk management outcome, not a strategy outcome.
