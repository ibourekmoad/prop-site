Why You Keep Failing Prop Firm Challenges (It's Not Your Strategy)

If you've failed a challenge or two, the temptation is to conclude your strategy isn't good enough. Usually that's the wrong diagnosis — and chasing it costs people months and a lot of challenge fees.

The three reasons

1. You size too big on the trade that matters

After a loss, position size quietly creeps up. Not deliberately — you just feel more strongly about the next setup, so the risk is bigger.

The result is that your biggest losses land on your worst days. Which is exactly backwards.

2. You don't know how much of today's allowance is gone

The daily loss limit resets, so it never feels urgent. Until you're 80% through it at 11am and take another trade anyway.

This is the single most common reason people fail. Not the total drawdown — the daily one.

3. You take one more trade to get back to flat

This is the revenge trade and it's fatal for a specific reason: at the moment you take it, your size is bigger, your rules are looser, and your patience is gone. All three at once.

Why "improve the strategy" doesn't fix these

None of those are entry problems. A better entry logic doesn't stop you oversizing after a loss. It doesn't tell you how much of today's allowance you've used. It doesn't stop the revenge trade.

What fixes them is boring: knowing the numbers before you click.

The unfair part

Prop firms don't fail you for losing. Losing is expected — that's the business. They fail you for breaking a rule.

So you can be right about the market and wrong about the rules, and the rules win.

What to do about it

Before every trade, three numbers in your head:

  • Risk per trade as a fixed percentage, every time. Not "whatever feels right".
  • Today's remaining allowance. If it's under half, reduce size or stop.
  • Total drawdown remaining. Know the gap between where you are and where you fail.

Then make the decision before you're emotional about it. Rules set in advance beat willpower every time.

Sizing, in one line

Position size = risk amount ÷ (stop distance × point value).

$100,000 account, 0.5% risk = $500. Entry 5200, stop 5195 = 5 points. One point is $5 per unit. So $500 ÷ $25 = 20 units.

Round down. Always down. Rounding up is how you silently end up risking 0.6% instead of 0.5% — and over 200 trades that leak is real money.

The uncomfortable truth

Nothing above is exciting. There's no indicator in it.

But the traders who pass challenges aren't the ones with the best entries. They're the ones still in the game on day 20. That's a risk management outcome, not a strategy outcome.
