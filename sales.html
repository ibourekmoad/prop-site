You didn't fail the challenge because your strategy is bad.

You failed because of a rule. And it's almost always the same one.

You failed because on day 12 you were already 80% through today's loss allowance and you took another trade anyway. Because you didn't notice your position size was three times what it should have been on the trade that mattered.

The strategy was never the problem. The risk tracking was.

The firm isn't judging your entries

They're watching three numbers. Break one and you're out, regardless of how good your trades were:

  • Daily loss limit — how much you can lose in one day. Usually 5%.
  • Max drawdown — total over the account. Usually 10%.
  • Consistency — whether one lucky day produced all your profit.

Most traders can't name all three. Then they wonder why the account is gone.

Questions people ask

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. Upload the file to Drive and open with Sheets. Everything calculates.

Does it work with futures firms like Apex and Topstep?

Yes, but those firms express limits in dollars per account size and use a trailing drawdown rather than a static one. Set your numbers carefully and treat the drawdown figure as a conservative guide. The daily loss limit and position sizer work exactly the same.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is a bookkeeping tool. Prop-firm rules change without notice — always confirm the numbers on your firm's own website before relying on them here. Trading carries risk of loss and nothing here guarantees you will pass a challenge or make money.

Will this make me profitable?

No. What it does is stop you losing money to arithmetic, oversizing, and not noticing how close you are to the daily limit. Those three things fail more challenges than bad entries ever will.
