Prop Firm Daily Loss Limit vs Max Drawdown — The Rule That Actually Ends Accounts

There are two loss limits on every prop-firm account and people confuse them constantly. One of them fails far more traders than the other, and it isn't the one you'd expect.

The two rules

Daily loss limit — how much you can lose in a single day before the firm fails the account. Usually around 5% of the starting balance. It resets at midnight (or at the firm's rollover).

Max drawdown — the total you can lose over the life of the account. Usually around 10%.

Break either one and the account is gone. But they behave completely differently, and that's where people get caught.

Why the daily limit is the dangerous one

Because it resets, it feels less serious. You have a bad morning, you use 70% of the allowance, and you think "it resets tonight anyway — one more trade to get back to even".

That trade is the one that ends it.

Three things compound at once: you size up because you're annoyed, your patience is gone, and you've left yourself almost no room for slippage or a gap. The daily limit doesn't care that it resets tomorrow. It only cares about right now.

A breach is never sudden

People imagine failing a challenge as one catastrophic trade. It's almost never that. It's a slow slide you didn't watch.

A typical pattern:

  • Small loss in the morning — fine
  • Slightly bigger trade because "this setup is better"
  • Third trade, now genuinely angry
  • Look at the dashboard: 90% of today's allowance gone
  • Fourth trade for the road

None of those decisions felt reckless individually. The total was fatal.

How to size around both

Work backwards from the daily limit, not forwards from a blog post that says "risk 1%".

If your daily limit is 5% and you risk 1% per trade, you get five attempts in a day. That sounds fine until you remember that three losses before lunch is a completely ordinary trading day.

At 0.5% per trade you get ten attempts. Ten bad trades in a row is genuinely unlikely. Three is common.

Use the free position size calculator to see what this does to your unit count. The maths is one line: risk amount divided by (stop distance × point value).

Trailing vs static drawdown

Worth knowing which one your firm uses, because it changes how you manage a winning open trade.

Static is measured from the starting balance and never moves. You know exactly where you stand.

Trailing follows your highest equity point. It moves up as you profit — but it does not move back down when you give gains back. Make $2,000 and the limit rises. Give it back and the limit stays where it rose to. That surprises people.

If your firm trails, treat the drawdown figure as stricter than it looks.

The rule I use now

If I've used 80% of today's loss allowance, I stop. Not "one more", not "this setup is different". Stop.

It has nothing to do with the quality of the next trade. It's about not handing the firm a reason to fail me on a technicality when my strategy was never the problem.

What actually helps

Knowing three numbers before every trade:

  • How much of today's loss allowance is left
  • How much total drawdown is left
  • The exact position size that keeps one loss to a fixed percentage

If you can't answer those in ten seconds, you're guessing. Guessing is what ends accounts.
