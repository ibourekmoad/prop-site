Position Sizing For A Prop Firm Challenge — The 10-Second Method

Position sizing is the least glamorous part of trading and the one that decides whether you pass the challenge. Here's the whole thing in one page.

The formula

Position size = risk amount ÷ (stop distance × point value)

Worked example:

| | |

|---|---|

| Account | $100,000 |

| Risk per trade | 0.5% → $500 |

| Entry | 5200 |

| Stop | 5195 |

| Stop distance | 5 points |

| Point value (MES) | $5 per point |

| Price risk per unit | 5 × $5 = $25 |

| Position size | $500 ÷ $25 = 20 units |

That's it. Run it in the calculator and you get the number instantly, plus your 1R and 2R targets.

Always round down

If the maths gives you 13.7 units, trade 13.

Rounding up is how you silently end up risking 0.6% instead of 0.5%. Each individual trade looks fine. Over 200 trades the leak is real money and you never notice it happening.

What to risk per trade

Common advice is 0.5–1%. The more useful answer is to work it out from your daily loss limit instead.

If the daily limit is 5% and you risk 1% per trade, you get five attempts a day. That sounds fine until you remember that three losses before lunch is an ordinary trading day.

At 0.5% you get ten attempts. Ten bad trades in a row is unlikely. Three is common.

So: half a percent per trade, or less. Not because it's optimal, but because it keeps you alive long enough for your edge to matter.

Fixed risk, every trade

Amateurs size by conviction — big when confident, small when nervous. That's backwards.

If a setup is good enough to take, it takes the same risk as every other setup. Two reasons:

  • Your statistics only mean something if the risk was consistent
  • Your "confidence" is worst precisely when it's highest — after a win

Same risk across instruments

When you switch from MES to NQ, the point value changes and so does your size. Re-calculate every time. Common point values:

| Instrument | $ per point |

|---|---|

| MES (Micro S&P) | $5 |

| ES (E-mini S&P) | $50 |

| MNQ (Micro Nasdaq) | $2 |

| NQ (E-mini Nasdaq) | $20 |

| MGC (Micro Gold) | $10 |

| GC (Gold) | $100 |

The stop is not decoration

If your stop is in the "I'll get out if it feels wrong" zone, you don't have a stop — and the whole calculation above is fiction.

A hard stop, entered at the same time as the entry. Then size from it. Three fields, no thinking.

Why this matters more in a challenge

On a personal account, oversizing costs you money slowly. On a challenge account it costs you the account, because there's a hard line you can't cross.

One trade at three times your planned risk is three losses of damage in one go. On a challenge with a 5% daily limit, that can be the whole day's room.

Where people get this wrong

  • Sizing by feel rather than by formula
  • Re-sizing after a loss (the size creeps up)
  • Rounding up instead of down
  • Forgetting to recalculate when switching instruments
  • No stop, or a stop they'd actually move

What the numbers can't tell you

Sizing keeps one loss to a fixed size. It does not tell you how much of today's loss allowance you've already used, or how close you are to failing on total drawdown.

That's the tracking problem, and it's what actually ends challenges.
