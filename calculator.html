The formula

Position size = risk $ ÷ (stop distance × point value)

Example: $100,000 account, 0.5% risk = $500. Entry 5200, stop 5195 = 5 points of risk. One point is $5 per unit, so the risk per unit is $25. $500 ÷ $25 = 20 units.

Why rounding down matters

If the maths gives you 13.7 units, trade 13. Rounding up is how you silently end up risking 0.6% instead of 0.5%. Over 200 trades that is real money, and it is the kind of leak you never notice because each individual trade looked fine.

Risk per trade vs daily loss limit

These are two different constraints and people confuse them constantly.

  • Risk per trade — how much one loss costs you. 0.5% is a sane default.
  • Daily loss limit — how much you can lose in one day before the firm fails you. Usually 5%.

If you risk 1% per trade and your daily limit is 5%, three losses in a morning and you are nearly done. At 0.5% you get ten attempts. Ten bad trades in a day is unlikely. Three is common.

Point values for common instruments

  • MES (Micro E-mini S&P) — $5 per point
  • ES (E-mini S&P) — $50 per point
  • MNQ (Micro E-mini Nasdaq) — $2 per point
  • NQ (E-mini Nasdaq) — $20 per point
  • MGC (Micro Gold) — $10 per point
  • GC (Gold) — $100 per point

For FX, use the dollar value of a full 1.00 move in the pair per unit you trade.
